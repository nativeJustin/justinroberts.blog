I finished reading Tactical Barbell last week, and overall I thought it was a pretty good book.

It's a fairly light read. The author keeps things simple, and none of the concepts are overly complicated. I actually appreciated that about it. You can get through the book pretty quickly and walk away with a good understanding of how to run the programs.

My biggest takeaway is that Tactical Barbell is basically a no-frills approach to building strength. You pick a cluster of compound lifts, stick with it for 12 weeks, and progressively increase the weight. That's really the foundation of the programming.

One of the programs in the book is called Operator, which is what I've decided to run for the next 12 weeks. The program has you training the same lifts three times a week, which means a lot of time under the bar practicing the same movements.

My current cluster consists of bench press, squats, and weighted pull-ups three times a week, with deadlifts once a week. I'm also throwing in some ring dips a couple of times a week.

I'm particularly interested in seeing how much my strength improves from training these movements so frequently. I've followed different strength programs over the years, but I like the idea of getting under the bar multiple times a week and having that many opportunities to practice the lifts with heavier loads.

The timing also works out pretty well. My wife and I recently moved back to Colorado, and I now have a squat rack set up in one of our extra bedrooms. I've got a pull-up bar and rings for dips, so I have pretty much everything I need. Having a home gym makes it a lot easier to train consistently without having to drive anywhere.

As far as conditioning goes, I'm doing Brazilian Jiu-Jitsu three mornings a week, which I think covers a lot of my high-intensity conditioning. I'm also planning to do an easy run once a week.

I'm currently reading Tactical Barbell II: Conditioning, and so far it seems pretty aligned with how I'm approaching things. Between Jiu-Jitsu, running, and lifting, I think I've got a pretty good balance without needing to add a bunch of extra work.

Overall, I enjoyed Tactical Barbell. It's easy to read, digestible, and doesn't try to make strength training more complicated than it needs to be.

I'm already running Operator, so I guess the real test will be seeing where my lifts end up after 12 weeks. I'm interested to see what kind of results I get from consistently training the same movements and spending so much time under the bar.

We'll see how it goes.
